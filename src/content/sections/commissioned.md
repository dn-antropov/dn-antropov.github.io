I work as an Unreal Engine developer, but generally I am tech agnostic and occasionally jump into Unity or Godot
and frequently venture into complex technical stacks including computer vision, artificial intelligence,
custom-made sensors, multi-screen setups and unusual haptics, or data-driven visualizations.
Due to rapidly changing nature of interaction design field where I spent most of my career, I used to 
such broad jumps between the applied spheres.

My expertise inside of Unreal Engine includes building gameplay systems, writing complex game logic, helping technical artists
with more complex tasks that might need c++ knowledge, constant performance optimization and occasional tooling.
I only worked with very small teams and on very rapidly moving projects so that I had to learn all those field the hard way
mainly in the middle of production with little sleep and much joy of exploration. It also taught me that the
performance is king and writing clean code is less important than delivering working results on time.

My heart was always into games, interactivity, and player experience. All these are possible only on a basis of performant
and optimized real-time systems. I believe that rapidly developing LLM technologies won't solve the challenges set by
these field and I don't rely on them in my work and try to learn everything the hard way.
